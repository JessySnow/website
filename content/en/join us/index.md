# What can you learn?
To build safe and reliable systems, it is essential not only to develop strong products (that is, programs) but also to properly design and maintain networks and development processes.
In our laboratory, we study these three elements—programs, networks, and development processes—as research targets. We place more emphasis on how to use natural language processing and machine learning technologies to solve real problems in practical system development than on simply improving the accuracy of those algorithms. At the same time, we apply cutting-edge techniques such as deep learning and natural language processing as part of our problem-solving approach. For this reason, our lab is a research area where students can learn advanced technologies while also acquiring the domain knowledge needed for software development.
This makes our lab a good fit for students who are considering careers as IT consultants, systems engineers, network engineers, or programmers.

# What skills will you gain from research?
**① Software development "good sense"**</BR>
You will acquire know-how and skills to derive more efficient and effective methods for important software development processes (design, implementation, quality testing, maintenance/operation) and infrastructure technologies such as the cloud. You will learn methodologies adopted by major companies and leading software development firms, and you will develop a cutting-edge sense for technology.

**② Mastery of cutting-edge trend technologies**</BR>
You will learn next-generation technologies that support society (AI, big data, cloud, IoT) as well as related foundational fields such as formal language theory, automata, process algebra, and graph theory. This builds practical abilities that can be applied in the real world.

**③ Global communication skills for taking on the world**</BR>
You can take on active research activities both domestically and internationally, making this lab a great fit for people who want to succeed in a global environment.

# What is the lab’s guidance policy?
We have established a student-centered supervision system that respects each student’s research interests. After joining the lab, multiple faculty members work together with each student over time to help them decide which field to pursue. From the perspectives of software development, AI/big data analysis, and cloud computing, we draw out each student’s latent interests and technical strengths and plan highly original research projects.

We also actively pursue collaborative research with overseas institutions, including universities in the United States, Canada, the Netherlands, Switzerland, Thailand, and other countries and regions. When appropriate, we send students on short-term internships to overseas or domestic research organizations so they can further improve their research outcomes. We encourage active external presentations of research results and aim for presentations at domestic research meetings, international conferences, and academic journals.

# What knowledge and skills are needed to begin research?
Our lab accepts students from a wide variety of backgrounds. However, to proceed efficiently in research, a strong interest in software mechanisms and software development is essential.
The following abilities are strengths for research. They are not mandatory before admission, but they are knowledge and skills we hope you will develop during the two years of the master’s program.

**① Programming skills**</BR>
You do not need advanced software development skills before admission, but programming skills are a powerful asset for producing research that can influence the world.

**② Basic mathematical literacy such as statistics**</BR>
Basic mathematical literacy in areas such as statistics is very useful for big data analysis and AI application.

**③ English speaking, reading, and writing skills**</BR>
Research is becoming increasingly global, and English communication skills are essential as international collaboration continues to grow.

# How should I prepare for the entrance exam?
To join our lab, you first need to pass the entrance exam. Studying for exam subjects such as mathematics and English is very important. At the same time, as shown on the [NAIST official page](http://isw3.naist.jp/Admission/nyushiQ_A-ja.html#:~:text=%E5%85%A5%E8%A9%A6%E3%81%A7%E3%81%AE%E9%85%8D%E7%82%B9%E3%81%AF,%E7%82%B9%E3%81%A7%E8%A9%95%E4%BE%A1%E3%81%95%E3%82%8C%E3%81%BE%E3%81%99%E3%80%82), the essay and interview account for the majority of the exam score.
Therefore, it is important to understand the research areas and trends covered by this lab. If you studied the same field during your undergraduate years, it may be easier, but most students do not. We recommend starting with the following.

#### 1. Learn about the research we do
If you are visiting this website, you have likely already read the research information posted here. However, this website only shows part of our work. The latest research is often not posted here because it cannot be made public on the website. First, try attending the open campus events held in February and May or the [Anytime Lab Visit](https://isw3.naist.jp/Admission/CampusTour-ja.html). During the Anytime Lab Visit, you can receive introductions to the latest research not shown on the website, consult about research themes after joining the lab, and talk casually with faculty and students. Many prospective students attend each year, and applications are accepted throughout the year. Online participation is also available, so remote visitors are welcome.

#### 2. Experience research firsthand (if you have time)
The open campus and Anytime Lab Visit are one-day events, so it may still be hard to imagine specific research themes. In that case, we recommend participating in the NAIST Summer Seminar held in August (applications open around July) or the Spring Seminar held in March (applications open around February). In these seminars, you can experience research work in our lab over two days under the guidance of our faculty.

For example, past seminar themes have included:
- Analyze network packets with machine learning to detect malicious attacks
- Introduction to modern software development operations (DevOps): software engineering techniques you can use today
- Experience next-generation no-code development that supports the future software industry — from AI and big data to RPA

You can also take a longer, optional research experience. Specifically, there are [Anytime Trial Enrollment](https://isw3.naist.jp/Admission/InternshipDomestic-ja.html) and [Internship](https://isw3.naist.jp/Admission/Internship-ja.html) programs (we recommend internships of one week or more). Both allow you to experience actual research and discuss your future research theme during the program. In the past, some participants continued research after the internship and even reached academic conference presentations.

Note that travel expenses are also provided for internships. Although the official page states applications should be submitted at least one month in advance, you may still be able to participate by consulting directly with our lab. Also, the seminars and internships do not require high levels of ability or practical skills from applicants (basic programming understanding is sufficient), so you can participate to learn about our lab’s research. We appreciate your consideration.
